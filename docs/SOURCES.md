## Every source line, by kind

### chapter

- ch-foundations · **Donella Meadows, Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System (1999)**: The twelve leverage points, ranked least to most effective. Read it for point 9 (lengths of delays) and point 6 (structure of information flows) — those two explain more failed security programs than any control framework does.
- ch-foundations · **Phil Venables, Leverage Points: A Cybersecurity Perspective**: A practitioner mapping of all twelve Meadows points onto security work, by someone who has run the function at Goldman Sachs and Google Cloud. Use it as the bridge between the systems literature and a real budget cycle.
- ch-foundations · **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (February 2024)**: Six functions now — GOVERN was added and placed at the centre of the wheel, touching Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. Twenty-two categories, one hundred and six subcategories. Good for structure and for a shared vocabulary with auditors; useless as a threat model.
- ch-foundations · **NIST SP 800-207, Zero Trust Architecture (August 2020)**: The seven tenets and the Policy Engine / Policy Administrator / Policy Enforcement Point split. The value is the decomposition, not the marketing term that grew on top of it.
- ch-foundations · **MITRE ATT&CK, Enterprise matrix**: The shared behavioural vocabulary. Note that v18 (October 2025) replaced per-technique Detections with Detection Strategies, and that the current Enterprise matrix carries fifteen tactic columns — Defense Evasion has been split into Stealth and Defense Impairment. If your coverage map still says fourteen tactics, it is stale.
- ch-foundations · **MITRE D3FEND (version 1.6.0 on 2026-11-04)**: The defensive counterpart to ATT&CK, organised as Model, Harden, Detect, Isolate, Deceive, Evict, Restore, over an ontology of digital artifacts. Use the artifact ontology to argue about what a control actually touches. ([source](https://d3fend.mitre.org/), read 2026-11-04)
- ch-foundations · **Center for Threat-Informed Defense, Summiting the Pyramid**: Grades a detection from Level 1 (ephemeral values) to Level 5 (core to the technique) and adds an observable axis: Application, User-Mode, Kernel-Mode, Payload, Header. This is how you stop lying to yourself about detection coverage.
- ch-foundations · **Hutchins, Cloppert and Amin, Intelligence-Driven Computer Network Defense (Lockheed Martin, 2011)**: The original intrusion kill chain paper. Read it for the actual argument — that defenders get repeated opportunities against a campaign, not one — rather than the seven-box picture everyone copies.
- ch-foundations · **David Bianco, The Pyramid of Pain**: The economic argument for TTP-level detection. Hash values and IPs cost the adversary minutes; tools cost weeks; TTPs force a redesign of the playbook.
- ch-foundations · **Threat Modeling Manifesto and Shostack's Four Question Framework**: Four questions: what are we working on, what can go wrong, what are we going to do about it, did we do a good job. The manifesto's anti-patterns — Hero Threat Modeler, Admiration for the Problem, Tendency to Overfocus, Perfect Representation — are a better review checklist than most methodologies.
- ch-foundations · **LINDDUN privacy threat modeling (linddun.org, 2024 knowledge-base update)**: Seven privacy threat types: Linking, Identifying, Non-repudiation, Detecting, Data Disclosure, Unawareness, Non-compliance. LINDDUN GO is the card-deck form that makes it usable in a one-hour session with engineers.
- ch-foundations · **Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report 2025**: 22,052 incidents and 12,195 confirmed breaches. Credential abuse 22 percent and vulnerability exploitation 20 percent of initial access, exploitation up 34 percent year over year, third-party involvement doubled from 15 to 30 percent. Use it for base rates, never for your specific risk.
- ch-foundations · **Mandiant M-Trends 2026**: Global median dwell time 14 days, up from 11. Internal detection 9 days versus 25 days for external notification, internal detection rate 52 percent. Exploitation the leading initial infection vector for the sixth consecutive year at roughly a third of intrusions.
- ch-foundations · **Google Project Zero, 0day In The Wild**: The tracker itself is useful; the caveat attached to it is essential. Project Zero notes that every entry is a failure case for an attacker, and that detection bias makes the true volume of in-the-wild zero-day an unknown. That single paragraph is the best short lesson on selection bias in security data anywhere.
- ch-identity · **NIST SP 800-63B-4, Digital Identity Guidelines: Authentication and Authenticator Management**: The only place to get the vocabulary right. Read the phishing-resistance definition (channel binding and verifier name binding), the AAL2 versus AAL3 split, the treatment of syncable authenticators, and why PSTN out-of-band is classed as restricted. Cite this, not a vendor blog, when someone claims their push app is phishing resistant.
- ch-identity · **NIST SP 800-207, Zero Trust Architecture**: Short, unglamorous, and the antidote to zero trust marketing. Use it for the PE/PA/PEP decomposition, the seven tenets, and the deployment-model taxonomy. Section 3 is the part vendors quote; section 5 (threats to a ZTA, including the risk of the policy engine itself) is the part they skip.
- ch-identity · **Lang, Czeskis, Balfanz et al., Security Keys: Practical Cryptographic Second Factors for the Modern Web (Financial Cryptography 2016) plus Google's subsequent internal deployment reporting**: The primary source behind the widely repeated claim that Google saw no successful phishing takeovers of employee accounts after mandating security keys across 85,000+ staff. It also has the usability data: authentication time cut substantially versus OTP, and second-factor authentication failures driven to zero. Good for convincing an exec that security and friction are not always trading.
- ch-identity · **Pang, Caceres, Burrows et al., Zanzibar: Google's Consistent, Global Authorization System (USENIX ATC 2019)**: The reference design for ReBAC at scale. Read it for zookies and the new-enemy problem, the Leopard index for deeply nested groups, and the honest latency and consistency tradeoffs. Everything sold as fine-grained authorization today is a descendant or a reaction to this paper.
- ch-identity · **Cutler, Dodds, Eidelman et al., Cedar: A New Language for Expressive, Fast, Safe, and Analyzable Authorization (arXiv 2403.04651)**: Why a deliberately non-Turing-complete policy language buys you static analysis — the ability to prove one policy never grants more than another. Read alongside OPA/Rego docs to understand what expressiveness costs you in reviewability.
- ch-identity · **Verizon 2025 Data Breach Investigations Report**: Use the initial-access-vector breakdown (credential abuse 22 percent, exploitation of vulnerabilities 20 percent, phishing 16 percent) and the infostealer findings on unmanaged devices. Best source for arguing budget in numbers an audit committee already trusts.
- ch-identity · **Google Cloud / Mandiant, UNC5537 Targets Snowflake Customer Instances for Data Theft and Extortion (2024)**: The cleanest modern case study in what happens when a data platform is protected only by a password. Roughly 165 organisations, credentials from commodity infostealers, no vendor breach involved. Use it to kill the argument that MFA on a service account is optional.
- ch-identity · **CISA/FBI joint advisory AA23-320A on Scattered Spider (UNC3944), updated July 2025**: Authoritative TTP list for help-desk social engineering, SIM swap, push bombing, adversary-in-the-middle kits, and abuse of federation by adding an attacker-controlled identity provider. The mitigations section is a ready-made control list for an identity programme.
- ch-identity · **Cyber Safety Review Board, Review of the Summer 2023 Microsoft Exchange Online Intrusion (Storm-0558)**: Read the whole thing once a year. Key-rotation decay, missing alerting on key age, a consumer key accepted for enterprise tokens, and detection that came from a customer rather than the vendor. It is the best public document on how key custody actually fails inside a competent organisation.
- ch-identity · **NIST FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), FIPS 204 (ML-DSA), FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA) and NIST IR 8547, Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards**: The standards plus the transition schedule. IR 8547 is the one to quote when someone asks for a date: it moves RSA-2048 and ECC P-256 to deprecated around 2030 and disallowed by 2035. CNSA 2.0 timelines bind national security systems earlier.
- ch-identity · **Böck, Zauner, Devlin, Somorovsky, Nonce-Disrespecting Adversaries: Practical Forgery Attacks on GCM in TLS (USENIX WOOT 2016)**: Empirical proof that nonce reuse in AES-GCM is not theoretical. They scanned the internet, found repeated nonces in production TLS stacks, recovered the authentication key and forged traffic. Show this to anyone who wants to generate nonces from a request counter shared across replicas.
- ch-identity · **SPIFFE and SPIRE project documentation (spiffe.io)**: The concrete answer to secret zero. SPIFFE ID format, X509-SVID versus JWT-SVID and why the project prefers the former (tokens replay, certificates bind to a channel), node and workload attestation, and automatic short-lived rotation.
- ch-identity · **AWS Key Management Service Cryptographic Details and the KMS Developer Guide**: Read the key-hierarchy section: domain keys rotated daily in HSM memory, HSM backing keys rotated yearly, derived encryption keys used once. It is the clearest public description of a production key hierarchy, and it makes precise what automatic rotation does and does not do to your existing ciphertext.
- ch-identity · **MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise, especially TA0006 Credential Access and T1556 Modify Authentication Process, together with MITRE D3FEND**: Use ATT&CK to enumerate how identity is actually attacked (forged web credentials, MFA request generation, steal application access token) and D3FEND to name the countermeasure you are proposing rather than describing it in prose.
- ch-detection · **Palantir — Alerting and Detection Strategy Framework (github.com/palantir/alerting-detection-strategy-framework)**: The ten-section template that turns a rule into an operable document: Goal, Categorization, Strategy Abstract, Technical Context, Blind Spots and Assumptions, False Positives, Validation, Priority, Response, Additional Resources. Use the Blind Spots section as your review gate; it is the part teams skip and the part that saves the on-call.
- ch-detection · **SigmaHQ — Sigma Rule Specification (github.com/SigmaHQ/sigma-specification)**: Authoritative on required fields (title, logsource, detection), condition semantics, value modifiers such as contains, startswith, endswith, re and exists, and the status and level vocabularies. Read it before you invent your own YAML dialect.
- ch-detection · **MITRE ATT&CK and ATT&CK Navigator (attack.mitre.org)**: Technique pages carry the procedure examples and named groups that make a detection concrete. Navigator is the layer format you should generate from your rule repository rather than colour in by hand.
- ch-detection · **MITRE Center for Threat-Informed Defense — Summiting the Pyramid**: The only widely used model that scores how hard an analytic is to evade, using levels from Ephemeral Values up to Core to (Sub-)Technique across Application, User-Mode and Kernel-Mode observation points. Version 4 added telemetry confidence scoring. This is what turns coverage from a colour into an engineering measurement.
- ch-detection · **Red Canary — Atomic Red Team (github.com/redcanaryco/atomic-red-team)**: Thousands of small, portable, cleanup-aware tests mapped to ATT&CK, driven by Invoke-AtomicTest. The right tool for detection unit-testing. Not an adversary emulation plan; do not confuse a technique test with a campaign.
- ch-detection · **MITRE Caldera (github.com/mitre/caldera)**: Agent-based automated adversary emulation with an Atomic plugin. Use it when you need a chained operation with sequencing and cleanup rather than isolated technique execution.
- ch-detection · **Google SRE Book, Chapter 6 — Monitoring Distributed Systems (sre.google/sre-book)**: The clearest statement anywhere of symptoms versus causes, the four golden signals, and the rule that a page must be urgent, actionable and require intelligence. Every alerting policy argument you will ever have is settled in this chapter.
- ch-detection · **Mandiant M-Trends 2026 (cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2026)**: Median dwell time 14 days, 122 days for espionage and North Korean IT worker cases, internal detection at 52 percent, exploits at 32 percent of initial infection vectors, hand-off to a second actor in 22 seconds. Use these to set your detection latency targets, not to decorate slides.
- ch-detection · **Verizon 2025 Data Breach Investigations Report**: Vulnerability exploitation as an initial access vector, edge device and VPN targeting growth, and remediation timelines for edge KEVs. Good for arguing that your detection strategy must assume the perimeter device is the entry point.
- ch-detection · **AWS — Logging data events with CloudTrail (docs.aws.amazon.com/awscloudtrail)**: States plainly that trails and event data stores do not log data events by default and that data events are billed separately. This single default explains most cloud investigations that dead-end at the bucket.
- ch-detection · **Amazon GuardDuty — IAM finding types (docs.aws.amazon.com/guardduty)**: Exact finding names and their data sources. Note the operational trap documented there: when GuardDuty observes continued activity from a remote host or account, the model learns it as expected and stops generating the finding.
- ch-detection · **Thinkst — Canarytokens (canarytokens.org and help.canary.tools)**: Free tripwires: DNS tokens, document tokens, AWS key tokens that fire on any API attempt. The lowest cost per unit of true positive in the entire discipline.
- ch-detection · **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (NIST CSWP 29) — Detect function**: DE.CM continuous monitoring and DE.AE adverse event analysis. The subcategory on declaring an incident when adverse events meet defined criteria is the one auditors ignore and the one that decides whether your program has a spine.
- ch-detection · **Alert Fatigue in Security Operations Centres: Research Challenges and Opportunities, ACM Computing Surveys (2025)**: The academic anchor for treating alert fatigue as a systems and human-factors problem rather than a discipline problem. Cite this when someone proposes to solve alert volume by hiring.
- ch-appsec · **OWASP Top 10:2025 — full category list and A03 Software Supply Chain Failures**: Read the methodology page before the list. The 2025 edition is eight data-driven categories plus two community-voted ones, and A03 was voted first by roughly half the community while appearing in a very small slice of the contributed CVE data. That gap between fear and measurement is the most instructive thing in the document.
- ch-appsec · **Google Security Blog — Eliminating Memory Safety Vulnerabilities at the Source (September 2024)**: The single best argument for safe-by-default language strategy. Android memory-safety bugs fell from 76 percent of vulnerabilities in 2019 to 24 percent in 2024 with almost no rewriting, because vulnerability density decays exponentially with code age. Use it whenever someone tells you the only options are rewrite or resign.
- ch-appsec · **The Chromium Projects — Memory Safety**: The base rate: around 70 percent of high-severity Chromium security bugs are memory-unsafety problems, from a study of 912 high or critical Stable-channel bugs since 2015, roughly half of them use-after-free. Also honest about the limits of sandboxing and site isolation, which matters when your answer to a bug class is containment.
- ch-appsec · **SLSA v1.1 specification — Build Track levels L0 to L3**: Short, precise, and explicit about scope. L1 provenance is trivially forgeable, L2 buys you signatures, L3 buys you isolation and signing keys that user-defined build steps cannot reach. Read the scope statements as carefully as the requirements: SLSA does not claim to tell you the source was good.
- ch-appsec · **Sigstore documentation — cosign, Fulcio, Rekor**: Keyless signing with ephemeral keys bound to an OIDC identity and logged in an append-only transparency log. The right mental model is that Sigstore answers who signed this and when, not is this software safe. Excellent for eliminating long-lived signing keys from CI.
- ch-appsec · **CISA Alert — Supply Chain Compromise of tj-actions/changed-files (CVE-2025-30066) and reviewdog/action-setup (CVE-2025-30154), March 2025**: The canonical mutable-tag incident. Version tags v1 through v45.0.7 were all repointed at one malicious commit that dumped runner memory into public build logs. Read it as a story about tags being pointers, not versions.
- ch-appsec · **Filippo Valsorda — A Retrospective Survey of 2024/2025 Open Source Supply Chain Compromises**: A working taxonomy of about twenty real compromises grouped by root cause: phishing, unsafe GitHub Actions triggers, credential theft, and control handoff. The most useful part is the conclusion, which reduces maintainer duty to a very short list including phishing-resistant authentication and never using pull_request_target.
- ch-appsec · **CrowdStrike — SUNSPOT: An Implant in the Build Process (SolarWinds)**: The implant watched for MsBuild processes, swapped a single source file for a backdoored copy while the compiler was running, and restored the original afterwards, with hash checks so builds would not fail and alert developers. This is the reference text for why your build machines are production.
- ch-appsec · **Codecov Security Update — Bash Uploader compromise (2021)**: A credential leaked through a Docker image layer let an attacker edit a script that thousands of CI pipelines curled and piped to bash. Undetected for roughly two months, and found by a customer verifying a checksum. Every clause of that sentence is a lesson.
- ch-appsec · **FIRST — Exploit Prediction Scoring System (EPSS)**: EPSS estimates the probability that a CVE will be exploited in the wild in the next thirty days, refreshed daily. Read the model and using-EPSS pages together so you understand it is a population-level probability, not a statement about your environment.
- ch-appsec · **CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog and Binding Operational Directive 22-01**: Inclusion requires a CVE ID, reliable evidence of active exploitation, and a clear remediation action. Treat KEV as ground truth about the past rather than prediction, and treat the federal due dates as a floor other regulators will eventually copy.
- ch-appsec · **Google OSS-Fuzz**: Continuous fuzzing at scale across roughly a thousand open-source projects, credited with finding well over ten thousand vulnerabilities and tens of thousands of bugs. The build integration documentation is the fastest path from zero to a running harness.
- ch-appsec · **GitHub Security Lab — Bugs that survive the heat of continuous fuzzing**: The necessary counterweight to OSS-Fuzz enthusiasm. Shows projects fuzzed for years at under twenty percent coverage, uninstrumented dependencies as blind spots, and encoders getting far less attention than decoders. Read it before you tell a board that a project is fuzzed.
- ch-appsec · **AWS EC2 documentation — instance metadata options and IMDSv2**: The concrete mechanics of the most common cloud SSRF escalation and its fix: session tokens obtained by PUT, a response hop limit, AMI and account-level defaults, and enforcement. Read the precedence table, because that is where teams believe they are enforced and are not.
- ch-incident · **NIST SP 800-61r3, Incident Response Recommendations and Considerations for Cybersecurity Risk Management (April 2025)**: Supersedes the 2012 r2 and deliberately kills the four-phase lifecycle picture. Use it for the argument that incident response is a risk-management property of the whole CSF 2.0 wheel, not a fire drill bounded by three days. Its two profile tables — preparation plus lessons learned, and detect/respond/recover — are the cleanest existing map of who owes what before an incident.
- ch-incident · **Google SRE, Incident Management Guide and the Managing Incidents chapter of the SRE Book**: Origin of the IC, operations lead, communications lead split and the three Cs — coordinate, communicate, control. Its single most useful rule is that the operations team is the only group permitted to modify the system during an incident. Steal that verbatim.
- ch-incident · **Building Secure and Reliable Systems, Chapter 17: Crisis Management (Google, O Reilly)**: The best free writing on why a security incident is not an outage: the adversary reacts to your response. Read it for operational security during response, out-of-band comms on freshly built hardware, parallel forensics and hunting workstreams, and the catalogue of ways teams accidentally tell the attacker they have been found.
- ch-incident · **CISA #StopRansomware Guide (joint CISA, FBI, NSA, MS-ISAC)**: The response checklist is the closest thing to a national standard for the first four hours of a ransomware event, including the isolate-before-power-off ordering that preserves memory. Good to hand directly to an infrastructure team that has never done this.
- ch-incident · **Sophos, The State of Ransomware 2025 (3,400 organizations, 17 countries)**: Best available survey numbers on the payment decision: roughly half paid, average payment about 85 percent of the initial demand, mean recovery cost excluding ransom around 1.53 million dollars, backup-based recovery at a six-year low. Use it to argue with executives who believe paying is fast.
- ch-incident · **Mandiant M-Trends 2025**: Dwell time by notification source is the number that changes budgets: eleven days median overall, twenty-six when an outside party tells you, five when the adversary tells you by encrypting things. Also the 2024 shift that put stolen credentials into second place as an initial vector. ([source](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/m-trends-2025), read 2026-11-04)
- ch-incident · **Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report, current edition**: Use for base rates when you are challenged on whether your scenario is realistic — particularly third-party and supply-chain involvement, and the steady prominence of edge-device exploitation. Read the methodology section before you quote a percentage at a board.
- ch-incident · **OFAC, Updated Advisory on Potential Sanctions Risks for Facilitating Ransomware Payments (Sept 2021, updating the Oct 2020 advisory)**: The controlling document for the payment decision in the United States. Civil liability is strict — intent is not a defense — and a self-initiated, timely and complete report to law enforcement plus ongoing cooperation is named as a significant mitigating factor. Print it and keep it in the crisis binder.
- ch-incident · **SEC Form 8-K Item 1.05 and the Division of Corporation Finance statement of 21 May 2024**: Four business days from the materiality determination, not from discovery, and the determination must be made without unreasonable delay. The 2024 staff statement pushed non-material incidents to Item 8.01 — the distinction is the whole exam question.
- ch-incident · **EU NIS2 Directive Article 23 and DORA Article 19 with the major-incident reporting RTS**: The clocks that beat GDPR: NIS2 at 24-hour early warning, 72-hour notification, one-month final report; DORA at four hours from classification as major and never more than 24 hours from awareness. If you operate in both regimes, DORA sets the binding constraint.
- ch-incident · **CrowdStrike, External Technical Root Cause Analysis — Channel File 291 (6 August 2024)**: A vendor post-incident report honest enough to be teachable: a template type defining twenty-one input fields against sensor code supplying twenty, a missing runtime array bounds check, a content validator logic error, and no staged deployment. The best available text for teaching that availability is a security property.
- ch-incident · **Okta Security, Unauthorized Access to Okta Support Case Management System: Root Cause and Remediation (Nov 2023)**: A service account credential saved into an employee personal Google account through Chrome profile sign-in, and a two-week detection gap caused by the file-access path emitting a different log event than the case-view path. The single best teaching case for logging blind spots and for why your customer told you first.
- ch-incident · **US House Energy and Commerce and Senate Finance testimony of UnitedHealth Group CEO Andrew Witty on the Change Healthcare attack (May 2024)**: Primary-source account of a Citrix remote access portal without multi-factor authentication, nine days of adversary presence before encryption, a 22 million dollar payment personally decided by the CEO, and a re-extortion afterward. - ch-incident · **In re Capital One Consumer Data Security Breach Litigation (E.D. Va. 2020), In re Rutter s (M.D. Pa. 2021), Wengui v. - ch-grc · **NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 (NIST CSWP 29, February 2024)**: Read the actual publication, not the vendor summaries. - ch-grc · **The Open Group Open FAIR Body of Knowledge: O-RT (Risk Taxonomy) and O-RA (Risk Analysis)**: The only normative definitions of the FAIR factors. - ch-grc · **Douglas Hubbard and Richard Seiersen, How to Measure Anything in Cybersecurity Risk, 2nd edition**: The one-for-one substitution argument: anywhere you use an ordinal scale, a simple probability model does better with the same inputs. - ch-grc · **Louis Anthony (Tony) Cox, What is Wrong with Risk Matrices?, Risk Analysis 28(2), 2008**: The mathematical demolition of the 5x5 matrix — range compression, ambiguous ordering, and worse-than-random performance when frequency and severity are negatively correlated. Cite it once in a governance meeting and the argument ends.
- ch-grc · **Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report 2025**: Third-party involvement in breaches doubled to thirty percent; exploitation of vulnerabilities as an initial vector rose thirty-four percent; ransomware present in forty-four percent of breaches with a median payment of one hundred fifteen thousand US dollars and sixty-four percent of victims not paying. - ch-grc · **FAIR Institute, FAIR-CAM (Controls Analytics Model)**: The missing physiology. - ch-grc · **PCI Security Standards Council, PCI DSS v4.0.1 and the future-dated requirements effective 31 March 2025**: Fifty-one of the sixty-four new requirements were future-dated to that date. - ch-grc · **CIS Critical Security Controls v8.1**: Best available answer to what should a small team do first. IG1 is the essential-hygiene subset. v8.1 added the Govern security function to align with CSF 2.0, which makes the crosswalk to a CSF Target Profile mechanical rather than interpretive.
